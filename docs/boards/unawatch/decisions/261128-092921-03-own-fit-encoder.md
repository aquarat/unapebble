# A FIT activity encoder of this project's own, not Garmin's SDK

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-28 09:29 UTC
- Decided by: agent

## Context
Strava, Garmin Connect, intervals.icu and most training tools import FIT
activity files. Garmin publishes the protocol (file header, definition and
data messages, the profile of message and field numbers, the CRC) and SDKs
in several languages. The SDKs' licence, the "Flexible and Interoperable
Data Transfer (FIT) Protocol License Agreement" (`LICENSE.txt` of
`garmin/fit-c-sdk`, read 2026-11-28), grants a non-transferable,
non-sublicensable licence "for Licensee's internal business purposes",
forbids distributing or making the licensed technology available to third
parties except as the agreement allows (2c), and forbids distributing it so
that it becomes subject to a licence requiring source disclosure or giving
others the right to modify it (2d). That is not compatible with vendoring it
into this Apache-2.0 firmware. Independent FIT readers and writers under
open licences exist (fitparse and fitdecode in Python, MIT).

## Decision
`pebble/apps/system/sport/fit.c` encodes activity files from the journal
(261128-092921-02), written from the protocol's public description, with no
Garmin source: the 14-byte header, little-endian definition messages, the
data messages file_id, event (timer start and stop), record, lap, session
and activity, and the CRC-16 of the protocol. Records carry the time,
position in semicircles, distance, speed, altitude, cadence (strides a
minute for running and walking, as the protocol has it), heart rate and
power when present. Paused seconds are not records. The manufacturer is
`development` (255). The encoder writes through a callback and runs twice
per file, once to size it for the header. Host tests decode its output with
fitdecode and fitparse, in a container.

## Consequences
No Garmin code or licence in the tree; the encoder is about the size of the
messages it writes. Whether every importer accepts the files is checked
against the public decoders here, and against the services by hand. The
agreement also names the protocol's documentation among the licensed
technology; this encoder only follows the published format, as the open
decoders do.
