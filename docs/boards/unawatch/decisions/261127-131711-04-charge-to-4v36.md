# Charge to 4.36 V: the cell is a 4.4 V one

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27 13:17 UTC
- Decided by: maintainer

## Context
261126-185344-01-charge-to-4v20 capped the charge at 4.20 V until the cell
was identified. The maintainer asked whether the firmware could charge to 4.40 V as the
stock firmware does. It can: the stock firmware charging to 4.40 V says the
cell is a 4.4 V (LiHV) one, made for it.
Supersedes 261126-185344-01-charge-to-4v20, and goes back to
261126-173552-04-charge-voltage.

## Decision
The default charge voltage is 4.36 V again
(`constant-charge-voltage-max-microvolt = <4360000>`, VBAT_REG code 0x26),
the earlier agent's choice: with the PCA9420's 0.75% it regulates at or below
4.393 V, never above the 4.40 V the stock firmware asks for, at a cost of
about 1 to 2% of the capacity. Exactly 4.40 V is there if the maintainer asks for
it. `CONFIG_CHARGER_PCA9420_CHARGE_LIMIT_MV` stays, off, for stopping
shorter.

## Consequences
More runtime than at 4.20 V, about 10% more, and a little more wear per cycle
than at 4.20 V, less than at 4.40 V. The battery test now measures the usable
capacity from 4.36 V (261126-190847-01-battery-test-phases, whose 4.20 V was
the cap of the time); results taken at 4.20 V are not comparable. The gauge
model follows (261127-131711-05-gauge-model-for-4v36).
