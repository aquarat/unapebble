# An STM32 SPI driver, interrupt driven, its pins undriven between transfers

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27 19:14 UTC
- Decided by: agent

## Context
The BlueNRG-2 is on SPI1 (261127-190543-01). The tree had a SPI class
(`pbl/drivers/spi.h`) with nRF5 and SF32LB57 drivers, none for the STM32.
The STM32U5's SPI is the STM32H7's: TSIZE, a FIFO of at least 8 bytes,
end of transfer (EOT). Its kernel clock, PCLK2 by default,
stops in Stop 2. The packets to the BlueNRG-2 are at most 260 bytes; the
stock firmware clocks it at 1.25 MHz with DMA.

## Decision
`drivers/spi/stm32.c`, for `st,stm32h7-spi`: master, 8-bit frames, software
slave management with GPIO chip selects, the clock the kernel clock over 2
to 256. A transfer is moved a byte at a time from the interrupt (RXP, TXP,
EOT), at most 8 bytes written ahead of those read, so the receive FIFO of
any instance never overruns; no DMA. The CPU stays out of Stop from the
bus being enabled to its being disabled, as the I2C driver does. With a
`sleep` pin state, the pins are put in it whenever the bus is disabled,
and in the default state when it is enabled; without one, AFCNTR keeps
them at their idle levels.

The board gives SPI1 an analog sleep state, so SCK, MOSI and MISO are not
driven while the chip is idle or held in reset (see
261127-191927-01-bluenrg2-hci-transport).

## Consequences
At 1.25 MHz a 260-byte packet takes about 2 ms of interrupts; DMA can come
later if throughput needs it. Selecting a pin state at every transfer
exposed that the STM32 pin controller took a port clock reference per pin
configured, which overflowed its count after about 64 transfers
(261127-193344-01-pinctrl-port-clock-once).
