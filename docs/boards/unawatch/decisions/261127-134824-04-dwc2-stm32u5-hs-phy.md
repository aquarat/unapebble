# The DWC2 driver on the STM32U5's high-speed PHY, by FIFO, powered while connected

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27 13:48 UTC
- Decided by: agent

## Context
PebbleOS's DWC2 driver knew the STM32H7's OTG HS (ULPI at high speed, or the
internal full-speed PHY). The U5A5's OTG HS has an internal UTMI high-speed
PHY, controlled from SYSCFG (OTGHSPHYCR) and supplied from PWR (USV,
USBPWREN, USBBOOSTEN), on the 16 MHz HSE. The stub brought it up, in slave
mode, and it enumerated at high speed on the watch.

## Decision
`st,stm32u5-hsotg` joins the driver (`drivers/udc/dwc2.c`, quirk
`DWC2_STM32U5_HSPHY`), with `st,syscfg` and `st,pwr` in the SoC's tree.
It follows the stub's sequence: PHY reference from the PHY clock's rate
(16 MHz), the transceiver supply, the PHY, the core reset, device mode, the
session valid by override (GCCFG VBVALEXTOEN and VBVALOVAL), high speed. It
moves packets through the FIFOs, as the stub does, not by DMA. At boot the
driver only takes down what was left (PHY off, supply off, core reset); the
PHY, its supply and the clocks (OTG, PHY, and so the HSE) come up at
`pbl_usbd_enable()` and go down at the new `pbl_usbd_disable()`. The
clock driver gains the PHY's reference multiplexer and lets the HSE stop.
CDC-ACM can leave connecting to the application (`USB_CDC_ACM_CONNECT`)
and gains a write that tells how much it took. FIFOs: receive 290 words,
endpoint 0 16, endpoints 1 to 4 128, the rest 32.

## Consequences
None of this has run in PebbleOS on a watch yet; the PHY steps are the
stub's, the rest is the driver as it runs on the H7. DMA on the U5 is left
for later.
