# A BMI270 driver of PebbleOS's own, with Bosch's configuration file

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27 19:48 UTC
- Decided by: agent

## Context
The maintainer asked for the watch's motion sensors to work in PebbleOS, so
that its own services (the accelerometer service for apps, shakes, health's
step counting and activity, the compass) run on the watch, the CPU staying in
Stop 2. The board had the simulator's accelerometer, which nothing feeds.

The BMI270 is on I2C4 at `0x68`, its INT2 on PA1, push-pull, active high, as
the stock firmware sets it (ImuBosch.cpp:541-557). Read on the watch: chip ID
`0x24`; with no configuration file loaded the part answers but its
accelerometer reads zeros (INTERNAL_STATUS 0). The stock firmware drives it
through Bosch's SensorAPI (86 KB of its own driver over about 1 MB of
Bosch's sources). PebbleOS's accelerometer class (`pbl/drivers/accel.h`)
wants a thin driver: batches of raw samples with their scale and axes,
shakes and double taps, and a peek.

## Decision
`drivers/imu/bmi270` is a driver of the class's own shape, after the
LSM6DSO's, without the SensorAPI. It loads Bosch's configuration file, the
8 KiB `bmi270_config_file` of SensorAPI v2.86.1 (BSD-3-Clause, credited in
NOTICE), unchanged, at init, and checks that the feature engine reports it
running. Then:

- The accelerometer only, the gyroscope off, in low power mode averaging 4
  samples (`CONFIG_ACCEL_BMI270_AVG_LOG2`), ±4 g (`CONFIG_ACCEL_BMI270_SCALE_MG`),
  with advanced power save on whenever the driver is not configuring the
  part or reading its FIFO, and 450 us after every access while it is on, as
  the SensorAPI waits. Found on the watch: in advanced power save, FIFO reads
  of 40 frames and more returned filler bytes (`0x90`, `0x8b`) and frames out
  of step, with the 450 us or without; with the power save off, reads of 125
  frames were clean. Turning it off and on again around a read costs two
  register writes a batch.
- Samples go through the FIFO, accelerometer frames without headers, read at
  its watermark interrupt, so the CPU wakes once a batch: at the
  accelerometer service's 25 Hz and 125 samples (`CONFIG_SERVICE_ACCEL_MANAGER_BATCH_SAMPLES`,
  as for the LSM6DSO), once every 5 s. The driver batches up to 128.
- Rates from 12.5 to 100 Hz, which are exact powers of two of 1600 Hz, the
  longest no longer than asked.
- Shakes are the feature engine's any-motion, which runs at 50 Hz: with
  shakes on the part samples at 50 Hz at least and the FIFO down-samples to
  the rate asked. The sensitivity maps 0 to 100% onto a threshold of 999 to
  32 mg, 2 samples long (`CONFIG_ACCEL_BMI270_SHAKE_*`). The engine gives no
  axis: the driver reports the axis that moved most since the last sample.
- No double tap: the base configuration file has none (the "legacy" one
  does, without the base's wrist features).
- Interrupts latched on the pin until their status is read; the work item
  runs again while the pad stays high, and a watchdog services the FIFO if
  its interrupt is more than half a batch late.

## Consequences
The class's consumers run on the real part; `imu accel` in the shell reads
it, `imu accel stream` takes its samples for a while and gives the rate,
mean and noise, and `bmi270` its statistics. The configuration file adds
8 KiB to the image and about 90 ms to the boot. Double taps need the legacy
configuration file, a choice between it and the base one's wrist gestures,
which nothing in PebbleOS uses yet.
