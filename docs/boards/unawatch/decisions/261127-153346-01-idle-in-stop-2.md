# Idle in Stop 2, out of it while the USB is powered or a transfer runs

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27 15:33 UTC
- Decided by: agent

## Context
The maintainer asked for PebbleOS to use the SoC's low-power modes on the
watch. PebbleOS's kernel is tickless (`CONFIG_CPU_IDLE`): when nothing runs
it picks the deepest idle state the SoC offers that no driver blocks and
that is worth entering before the next timeout. The STM32U5 offered only
Sleep (WFI), with the CPU on PLL1 at 160 MHz: measured on the gauge
(261127-150636-01), the watch drew 6.3 mA idle in the launcher, the USB
held off, 99% of the time in WFI. The stock firmware idles in Stop 2
(hardware.md, section 1.5).

The system timer is LPTIM4 on the LSE; LPTIM1 and LPTIM3 clock the
display's VCOM. All three are in the SmartRun domain and the clock driver
already sets their autonomous mode (SRDAMR), so they count on and LPTIM4
wakes the CPU in Stop 2; the EXTI lines of the buttons and the PMIC wake it
too. Stop 2 keeps the SRAMs and the registers, and stops every other clock.
Stop 3 would also stop the LPTIMs.

## Decision
The STM32U5's second idle state is Stop 2 (`SOC_STM32_IDLE_STOP`), with the
devicetree's 500 us exit latency and 10 ms minimum residency. On the way out
it restores what Stop took down, as the stock firmware does: the core supply
range and the EPOD booster, MSIS at its range (Stop wakes it at 24 MHz at
most), the oscillators that ran, PLL1, then the system clock. The flash wait
states, dividers and PLL1 settings are kept by the hardware.

Peripherals that stop with their clock block Stop while they work: the USB
OTG HS while it is powered (its PHY and the HSE), an I2C bus during a
transfer, and, as their drivers already did, the eMMC during its power-up
and each transfer (an idle card stays powered through Stop until its 2 s
power-off) and the USARTs while receiving or sending.

## Consequences
With the USB developer access connected the CPU stays in Sleep, as before:
the console and the tether keep working. Without it, the watch idles in
Stop 2 about 98% of the time; the numbers are in the board page's Power
section. Every wake-up from Stop relocks PLL1, tens of microseconds. A
driver for a peripheral outside the SmartRun domain that works while the
CPU waits (SPI, DMA, timers other than the LPTIMs, the RNG) must block Stop
while it does.
