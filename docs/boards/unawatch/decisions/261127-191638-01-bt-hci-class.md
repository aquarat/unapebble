# The BlueNRG-2 as a bt_hci device, the transport in NimBLE's transports

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-27 19:16 UTC
- Decided by: agent

## Context
NimBLE's HCI transports live in `third_party/nimble/transport`, one per
kind of controller, and reach their hardware through a role: the UART of
`hci_uart.c` (`bt_hci_uart`), the LCPU of `hci_sf32lb.c`
(`bt_controller`, a remoteproc). The BlueNRG-2 is a SPI device with two
more lines, IRQ and RESETN. Devices come from the devicetree, through a
spec in `drivers/`, `kernel/` or `soc/` and a class. Linux has no binding
for the part; Zephyr calls the protocol `st,hci-spi-v2`, and the tree's
rule is a compatible per part.

## Decision
A binding `st,bluenrg-2` (`dts/bindings/net/bluetooth`): `reg`,
`spi-max-frequency`, `spi-cpha`, `irq-gpios`, `reset-gpios`. A small
class, `bt_hci` (`pbl/drivers/bt_hci.h`), whose devices only describe a
controller for its transport, with the role `bt_hci` needed by
`NIMBLE_HCI_BLUENRG2`. The BlueNRG-2's device (`drivers/bt_hci/bluenrg2.c`)
holds its SPI device, IRQ and RESETN, and at init puts the chip in reset
and IRQ as an input. The protocol is in the NimBLE transport,
`hci_bluenrg2.c`, as on the other boards.

## Consequences
The board says where the chip is (`unawatch.dts`, `roles.yml`); the lab
variant keeps the fake controller and builds no BlueNRG-2 device. With the
fake controller selected instead, the board still drives the BLE reset low
at boot.
